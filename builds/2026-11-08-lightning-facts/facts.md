# 5 lightning "facts." Only 1 is true. — fact sheet

Format: myth vs fact (5 claims, 4 myths + 1 true, plus a bonus rule).

| On screen | Source | What the source says |
|---|---|---|
| The Empire State Building: hit about **25 times** a year | MPR News, "When lightning strikes tall buildings" (Paul Huttner, Jul 8 2014), quoting the National Weather Service — https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/07/08/when-lightning-strikes-tall-buildings ; Live Science, "Video Captures Lightning Striking Empire State Building 3 Times" (Brett Israel, Apr 13 2011) — https://www.livescience.com/13704-empire-state-building-lightning-strike.html | MPR: "According to the National Weather Service, the Empire State Building is hit by lightning an average of 25 times per year." Live Science: "about 20 to 25 times each year, on average" (lightning expert Martin Uman) and "25 strikes a year is average". |
| Once, it got hit **8 times** in **24 minutes** | Live Science (2011); MPR News (2014), same URLs | Live Science: "The building was once struck eight times in 24 minutes". MPR: hit eight times in one storm, within 24 minutes. |
| Rubber tires give **NO** protection | NWS Fort Worth, "Myths vs. Facts" — https://weather.gov/fwd/ltgmythvfact | "Rubber-soled shoes and rubber tires provide NO protection from lightning." |
| A hard-top car's **steel frame** is what gives protection | NWS Fort Worth, same URL | "However, the steel frame of a hard-topped vehicle provides increased protection if you are not touching metal." |
| Lightning can strike **10 miles** from any rain | NWS Fort Worth, same URL | "Lightning often strikes outside of heavy rain and may occur as far as 10 miles away from any rainfall." |
| It often strikes **outside** heavy rain | NWS Fort Worth, same URL | Same sentence. |
| "Heat lightning" is lightning **too far away** to hear | NWS Fort Worth, same URL | "What is referred to as "heat lightning" is actually lightning from a thunderstorm too far away for thunder to be heard." |
| Thunder only carries about **10 miles** | NWS, "Understanding Lightning: Thunder" — https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-thunder | "can be heard for a distance of only about 10 miles from the lightning strike." |
| It can heat the air to **50,000°F** | NWS, "How Hot Is Lightning?" — https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-temperature ; NWS thunder page | "lightning can heat the air it passes through to 50,000 degrees Fahrenheit (5 times hotter than the surface of the sun)." |
| That's **5×** hotter than the Sun's surface | NWS, same two pages | Same sentence. |
| Every **5 seconds** = **1 mile** away | NWS thunder page | "it takes the sound of thunder about 5 seconds to travel a mile" … "5 seconds = 1 mile, 15 seconds = 3 miles, 0 seconds = very close." "Keep in mind that you should be in a safe place while counting." |
