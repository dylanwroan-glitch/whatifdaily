# Pour Hoover Dam all at once. What happens? — fact sheet

Format: simulation ("what actually happens if…", with the real numbers). Every figure comes from the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Reclamation), which built and runs Hoover Dam.
Quotes below were read from the source pages on 2026-11-12. The canyon, dam, cableway, ice plant, oven and bread are stylized 3D models, not survey-accurate geometry.

Sources:
- **BOR-C**: Reclamation, "The Story of Hoover Dam — Essays: Concrete" (last updated 3/12/15) — https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/History/essays/concrete.html
- **BOR-F**: Reclamation, "Hoover Dam — Frequently Asked Questions and Answers" (last updated 3/12/15) — https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/faqs/damfaqs.html
- Supporting: Reclamation's "Boulder Dam" facts booklet as reprinted by NPS History — https://www.npshistory.com/publications/burec/boulder_dam2/facts.htm

## Scenario logic and stylization (not presented as fact)
- The single-pour scenes are a simulation of Reclamation's calculation, labelled "SIMULATION" on screen. The false-colour "thermal view", the cooling front that creeps inward over the 125 years and the crack pattern are illustrations, not a heat model of the real dam.
- The dam is modelled as a curved arch-gravity wall about 726 ft tall and very thick at the base; the columns are drawn as a grid of blocks staggered in 5-foot lifts, the way construction photos show them. Column sizes, coil spacing and pipe layout are stylized.
- The cableway bucket, the ice plant on the lower cofferdam and the oven are illustrations. No temperatures are shown because the sources give none.
- The bread pile is a visual gag for the 500,000-loaves-a-day figure; it is not drawn to scale.

## Checked and not used
- Total concrete for the whole project: BOR-F says 4,360,000 cubic yards in one place and 4,400,000 in another (the booklet also says 4,400,000), so only the 3.25 million cubic yards "in the dam" figure is used.
- The booklet's highway comparison runs "from Miami, Fla., to Seattle, Wash." while BOR-F and BOR-C say San Francisco to New York City; the video uses the San Francisco–New York version from the two current Reclamation pages.
- Last concrete placed (BOR-F: May 29, 1935) is later than "Cooling was completed in March 1935"; to avoid a confusing pair of dates only the cooling date is shown.
- Whether the concrete is "still curing" today: neither Reclamation page addresses it, so the video doesn't claim it.
